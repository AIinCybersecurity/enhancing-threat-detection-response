Abstract

This research project examines the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in detecting and preventing cyber threats, focusing specifically on the context of Bahrain[cite: 1, 430]. As Buczak and Guven (2016) point out, modern cyberattacks are growing highly complex, making it virtually impossible to rely on manual human effort alone for threat detection and mitigation[cite: 2, 431]. To explore this, we used a mixed-methods approach, combining a thorough review of existing academic literature with empirical data from a survey of 250 IT students and professionals in Bahrain[cite: 4, 433]. Our survey data shows that respondents hold a generally positive attitude toward the integration of AI in security operations, with an overall attitude mean score of 3.64 out of 5.00[cite: 6, 435]. Although 60% of the respondents view AI as an essential asset, they also highlighted significant implementation challenges regarding algorithmic transparency and accuracy[cite: 7, 8, 436, 437].

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Research Background

In this section, the basics of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in cybersecurity are introduced. This is about facilitating real-life applications of AI's impact on digital threat detection and response[cite: 598, 599, 600]. This background aims to make readers aware of these circumstances to provide them with background for the appreciation of the current study and the context in which it was done[cite: 601].

1.1.1 Overview of Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is described by NIST (2023) as the process of protecting computers, systems, networks, and data from attacks or any damage, thereby ensuring uninterrupted functioning and safety[cite: 604]. NIST (2023) noted that the primary objective of cybersecurity is to keep the CIA triad, including confidentiality, integrity, and availability[cite: 606]. Buczak and Guven (2016) reported that cyber threats have evolved and become harder to manage, with phishing, malware, and ransomware becoming more widespread[cite: 610, 611].

1.1.2 Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

Early AI systems were predominantly rule-based, requiring developers to manually program all logic; however, they broke down when presented with novel or unexpected inputs[cite: 615, 616]. According to Shone et al. (2018), this changed with machine learning, which enables systems to learn through data rather than following instructions[cite: 617, 618]. Nallapareddy and Katta (2025) noted this represents a shift from signature-based threats to learning and adapting[cite: 621].

1.1.3 Role of AI in Cybersecurity

AI is used for several specific jobs, most notably threat detection; AI systems can more quickly and effectively process large amounts of data[cite: 624, 625]. This is something a human analyst wouldn't be able to do in a big organization with thousands of events per second[cite: 626]. AI can help determine threat severity and automatically respond by isolating compromised devices[cite: 628]. It also helps security teams reduce "noise" from false alerts[cite: 630, 631, 632].

1.2 Research Problem

Problem Statement: Cybersecurity threats have become more sophisticated, faster, and harder to detect using traditional defence mechanisms[cite: 640]. Conventional security systems often rely on static rules and reactive responses, making them insufficient against modern attacks such as zero-day exploits and APTs[cite: 641]. While AI offers promising capabilities, organizations face uncertainties regarding accuracy, reliability, and ethical implications[cite: 642, 643].

1.4 Research Objectives

  • To understand how modern AI and machine learning improve the speed and accuracy of detecting cyber threats compared to older methods[cite: 31, 654].
  • To explore how AI can help respond to attacks in real time, including learning from new threats as they happen[cite: 32, 655].
  • To study ways to make AI’s decisions more transparent and understandable for security teams[cite: 33, 656].
  • To identify challenges like attackers trying to fool AI systems and find ways to make AI-based security stronger[cite: 34, 657].

1.5 Research Significance

This research is significant because existing systems rely on fixed signatures or rules, a strategy that fails against new attack types[cite: 35, 36, 659, 660]. AI-based IDS systems process vast amounts of network traffic faster than human counterparts, providing security personnel more time for responding[cite: 38, 662]. Results can assist Bahrain and other organizations in the Gulf region in making better choices on how to utilize AI within security systems[cite: 669].

1.6 Gaps Analysis

Shone et al. (2018) and Tang et al. (2020) discovered that while research focuses on detection accuracy, it often fails to consider practical problems of deployment within an organization[cite: 672]. Capuano et al. (2022) found a lack of "explainability" and trust; if security analysts cannot understand why a system flags a threat, they cannot rely on it[cite: 674, 675]. Finally, there is not enough research focusing on human and organizational issues, such as the willingness of security teams to embrace AI[cite: 681].

Chapter 4: Methodology

4.1 Introduction

This chapter serves as the roadmap of procedures employed to determine the influence of artificial intelligence on improving cybersecurity[cite: 111, 500]. A questionnaire survey was used to collect quantitative data, opinions, and knowledge from participants[cite: 112, 500].

4.2 Research Strategy & Philosophy

We used a quantitative strategy applied through surveys to measure awareness levels consistently based on standard scales[cite: 116, 117, 501]. Epistemologically, the research uses a positivist approach, where knowledge is gained through measurable information and objective observations using a 5-point Likert scale[cite: 124, 125, 127, 503].

4.4 Sample Selection

The target population includes university students (Information Systems, Computer Science, Engineering) and IT professionals in Bahrain[cite: 163, 164, 509]. We used convenience sampling via WhatsApp groups of students and IT personnel[cite: 156, 157, 166, 510]. Data collection was conducted from 1st November 2025 to 1st May 2026, ensuring all responses were compiled before the May 12th deadline[cite: 190, 199, 512, 515].

4.5.2 Questionnaire Structure

The questionnaire was categorized into four parts: Section A (Demographics), Section B (General Awareness of AI and Cybersecurity), Section C (Seven constructs including AI Threat Detection, Incident Response, and Technical Features), and Section D (Overall Opinions)[cite: 182, 183, 184, 186, 202, 209, 213, 514].

Chapter 5: Data Analysis and Results

Demographic results showed 49.6% of respondents were from the IT field, and 60.4% were Bachelor's degree students[cite: 291, 523, 526, 527]. Reliability analysis using Cronbach’s Alpha showed high consistency (0.897 to 0.935)[cite: 306, 530]. Regression analysis confirmed that H5 (Perceived Usefulness) and H6 (Perceived Ease of Use) were the primary drivers of effectiveness, while individual technical predictors (H1-H4) were not statistically significant on their own due to multicollinearity[cite: 108, 109, 350, 351, 542, 547, 548].

Chapter 6: Conclusion

The research confirms that the role of AI in improving cybersecurity within Bahrain is viewed positively[cite: 357, 553]. However, its potential depends critically on how professionals interact with these tools; organizations must ensure systems are not only technically capable but also user-friendly and transparent[cite: 355, 356].
