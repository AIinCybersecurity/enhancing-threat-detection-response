Abstract

This research project examines the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in detecting and preventing cyber threats, focusing specifically on the context of Bahrain[cite: 403]. As Buczak and Guven (2016) point out, modern cyberattacks are growing highly complex, making it virtually impossible to rely on manual human effort alone for threat detection and mitigation[cite: 404]. To explore this, we used a mixed-methods approach, combining a thorough review of existing academic literature with empirical data from a survey of 250 IT students and professionals in Bahrain[cite: 406]. Our survey data shows that respondents hold a generally positive attitude toward the integration of AI in security operations, with an overall attitude mean score of 3.64 out of 5.00[cite: 408].

Keywords: Bahrain, UOB, AI, Cybersecurity, Threat Detection, Mixed-Methods Research[cite: 412].

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Research Background

In this section, the basics of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in cybersecurity are introduced, focusing on facilitating real-life applications of AI's impact on digital threat detection and response[cite: 572, 573].

1.1.1 Overview of Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is described by NIST (2023) as the process of protecting computers, systems, networks, and data from attacks or any damage, ensuring uninterrupted functioning and safety[cite: 577]. The primary objective is to maintain the CIA triad: confidentiality, integrity, and availability[cite: 579].

1.1.2 Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

Early AI systems were predominantly rule-based, but the introduction of machine learning changed everything by enabling systems to learn from data rather than just following instructions[cite: 588, 591].

1.2 Research Problem

Cybersecurity threats have become more sophisticated and harder to detect using traditional mechanisms which rely on static rules and manual monitoring[cite: 613, 614]. This research seeks to identify factors influencing the successful implementation of AI in these systems[cite: 618].

1.4 Research Objectives

  • To understand how AI improves the speed and accuracy of detecting threats[cite: 627].
  • To explore real-time attack response and learning capabilities[cite: 628].
  • To study ways to make AI decisions more transparent for security teams[cite: 629].
  • To identify challenges like adversarial AI and strengthen dependability[cite: 630].

Chapter 2: Literature Review

AI is used for several specific jobs in cybersecurity, most notably threat detection and incident response[cite: 597, 600]. AI-based IDS systems process vast amounts of network traffic faster than humans[cite: 635]. However, gaps exist regarding the practical problems of deployment and the lack of explainability and trust in automated systems[cite: 645, 647].

Chapter 3: Research Model and Hypotheses

The research model examines Independent Variables such as AI-based detection and response capabilities, and Moderating Variables like perceived usefulness and ease of use[cite: 463, 465].

  • H1: AI-based Threat Detection Capabilities[cite: 467].
  • H5: Perceived usefulness of AI[cite: 520].
  • H6: Perceived ease of use[cite: 521].

Chapter 4: Methodology

This study used a quantitative strategy applied through surveys to measure the level of awareness and attitudes regarding AI and cybersecurity[cite: 99, 100].

4.4.3 Sample Size

A total of 250 valid responses were received from university students and IT practitioners in Bahrain[cite: 142, 160]. Data collection occurred from November 1st, 2025 to May 1st, 2026 via Google Forms[cite: 173, 174].

Chapter 5: Data Analysis and Results

The total mean for AI awareness was 3.50[cite: 292]. The results indicate that while technical capabilities are strong, the effectiveness of AI depends critically on how useful and easy to work with professionals perceive the tools to be[cite: 349]. Regression analysis showed H5 (Perceived Usefulness) and H6 (Ease of Use) were statistically supported.

Chapter 6: Conclusion

The research confirms that AI's role in improving cybersecurity in Bahrain is viewed positively[cite: 351]. Organizations should invest not just in the technology, but in ensuring end-users find it easy to integrate into existing workflows[cite: 350].
