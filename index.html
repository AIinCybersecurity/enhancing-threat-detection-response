Abstract

This research project examines the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in detecting and preventing cyber threats, focusing specifically on the context of Bahrain[cite: 412]. As Buczak and Guven (2016) point out, modern cyberattacks are growing highly complex, making it virtually impossible to rely on manual human effort alone for threat detection and mitigation[cite: 413]. This shift makes the development of automated, intelligent systems a necessity[cite: 414]. To explore this, we used a mixed-methods approach, combining a thorough review of existing academic literature with empirical data from a survey of 250 IT students and professionals in Bahrain[cite: 415]. Our survey data shows that respondents hold a generally positive attitude toward the integration of AI in security operations, with an overall attitude mean score of 3.64 / 5.00[cite: 417]. Although 60% of the respondents view AI as an essential asset, they also highlighted significant implementation challenges[cite: 418].

Keywords: Bahrain, UOB, AI, Cybersecurity, Threat Detection, Mixed-Methods Research[cite: 421].

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Research Background

In this section, the basics of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in cybersecurity are introduced, facilitating real-life applications of AI's impact on digital threat detection and response[cite: 581, 582].

1.1.1 Overview of Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is described by NIST (2023) as the process of protecting computers, systems, networks, and data from attacks or any damage, ensuring uninterrupted functioning and safety[cite: 586]. The primary objective is to maintain the CIA triad: confidentiality, integrity, and availability[cite: 588]. Buczak and Guven (2016) reported that cyber threats have evolved and become harder to manage, with phishing, malware, and ransomware becoming more widespread[cite: 592, 593].

1.1.2 Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

Early AI systems were predominantly rule-based, requiring manual programming[cite: 597]. This changed with machine learning, enabling systems to learn from data[cite: 599, 600]. Deep learning utilizes neural networks to process complex data like image or speech data[cite: 601]. This represents a shift from signature-based threats to learning and adapting[cite: 603].

1.3 Research Questions

  • What are the factors affecting the effective adoption of AI as a threat detection and response in cybersecurity? [cite: 630]
  • How do these factors influence the capability of AI systems to detect, analyse, and respond to cyber threats? [cite: 631]
  • How do organizational knowledge and team attitudes affect adoption intention? [cite: 632]

1.4 Research Objectives

  • To understand how AI improves the speed and accuracy of detecting cyber threats[cite: 636].
  • To explore how AI can help respond to attacks in real time[cite: 637].
  • To study ways to make AI’s decisions more transparent and understandable[cite: 638].
  • To identify challenges like adversarial attacks and find ways to make AI stronger[cite: 639].

Chapter 2: Literature Review

AI is used for threat detection; systems can process large amounts of data faster than human analysts[cite: 607, 608]. AI can identify known and unknown attack types, including zero-day attacks[cite: 674]. However, gaps exist in practical deployment and the lack of "explainability" in black-box models[cite: 654, 656]. Guidotti et al. (2018) warned regarding the lack of transparency and risk of human over-reliance on automation[cite: 419].

Chapter 3: Research Model and Hypotheses

The framework focuses on AI-driven threat detection models and incident response frameworks[cite: 668]. This study focuses on six key constructs: AI-based threat detection (H1), incident response (H2), technical features (H3), knowledge and skills (H4), usefulness (H5), and ease of use (H6)[cite: 241, 353].

Chapter 4: Methodology

This research used a mixed-methods approach[cite: 702]. Quantitative data was collected via a Google Forms survey distributed through WhatsApp to students and IT professionals in Bahrain[cite: 703, 704, 231]. Data collection occurred from November 1, 2025 to May 1, 2026[cite: 202]. The final analysis was conducted on 250 valid responses[cite: 238].

Chapter 5: Data Analysis and Results

The total mean for AI awareness was 3.50[cite: 316]. Regression analysis confirmed that H5 (Perceived Usefulness) and H6 (Perceived Ease of Use) were statistically supported[cite: 333]. The results suggest that the effectiveness of AI depends critically on how security professionals perceive and interact with these tools[cite: 358].

Chapter 6: Conclusion

The research confirms that AI's role in improving cybersecurity within Bahrain is viewed positively[cite: 360]. Organizations must ensure that systems are not only technically capable but also user-friendly to realize their full potential[cite: 359].
